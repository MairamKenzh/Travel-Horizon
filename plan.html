Plan Your Trip

Everything a foreign visitor needs to know before arriving in Kyrgyzstan.

🛂 Visa & entry

  • Citizens of 60+ countries (EU, UK, USA, Canada, Australia, Japan, Korea and others) can stay visa-free for up to 60 days.
  • Other nationalities can apply online for an e-Visa at evisa.e-gov.kg.
  • Your passport should be valid for at least 6 months.
  • Some border areas (e.g. near China and Tajikistan) need a special border permit. Arrange it through a local agency.

Rules can change, so always confirm with the embassy of Kyrgyzstan in your country before travelling.

✈️ Getting there

  • Manas International Airport (FRU), 30 km from Bishkek, is the main gateway.
  • Direct flights from Istanbul, Dubai, Moscow, Almaty, Tashkent, Delhi, Ürümqi and more.
  • Osh Airport (OSS) serves the south of the country.
  • Overland: Almaty (Kazakhstan) to Bishkek is only about 4–5 hours by bus or shared taxi.

🚐 Getting around

  • Marshrutkas (minibuses) connect almost every town. They are cheap and frequent.
  • Shared taxis leave from bus stations when full. They are faster than buses.
  • In cities use taxi apps like Yandex Go to get a fixed price.
  • For remote areas (Song-Kul, Tash-Rabat), hire a driver or a 4×4 tour.
  • Domestic flights link Bishkek and Osh in about 1 hour.

💵 Money

  • Currency: Kyrgyz som (KGS).
  • ATMs are common in Bishkek, Osh and Karakol, but rare in villages, so carry cash.
  • Cards work in hotels, supermarkets and many restaurants in cities.
  • Exchange offices give better rates than airports. Bring clean, new USD or EUR notes.

📱 Internet & SIM

  • Buy a local SIM at the airport or in town: Beeline, MegaCom or O!
  • You need your passport to register a SIM.
  • 4G covers cities and main roads; mountain areas often have no signal.

🔌 Good to know

  • Electricity: 220 V, plug types C and F (European).
  • Time zone: UTC +6, no daylight saving.
  • Tap water: drink bottled or filtered water.
  • Emergency: 112 (police 102, ambulance 103, fire 101).

When to go

Best time to visit

Spring

April – May

Green valleys and blooming apricot trees. Mountain passes may still be snowy.

Summer

June – September

Peak season. Yurt camps open, high lakes are accessible, and Issyk-Kul is warm enough to swim.

Autumn

September – October

Golden colours, harvest season and fewer tourists. Nights get cold in the mountains.

Winter

December – March

Skiing in Karakol, freeriding and snowy landscapes. Many high areas are closed.

Safety & FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is Kyrgyzstan safe for tourists?
Yes. Kyrgyzstan is generally considered safe and welcoming for visitors. Use normal precautions: watch your belongings in bazaars, use official taxis at night, and register with your embassy for longer trips.
Do people speak English?
English is spoken in hotels, tour agencies and by many young people in Bishkek. In the countryside, Russian or Kyrgyz is more common, so a translation app and a few local phrases help a lot.
Do I need a guide for trekking?
Popular trails like Ala-Archa or Karakol–Ala-Kul can be hiked independently in summer. For remote routes, glaciers or multi-day horse treks, a local guide is strongly recommended. Weather in the mountains changes fast.
What about altitude sickness?
Many destinations are above 2,500 m. Ascend gradually, drink plenty of water and avoid alcohol on the first days at altitude. Descend if you get a strong headache, nausea or dizziness.
Do I need travel insurance?
Yes, we strongly recommend insurance that covers medical evacuation and adventure activities like trekking and horse riding.
